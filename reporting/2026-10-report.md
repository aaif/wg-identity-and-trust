# [Report] Identity & Trust – October 2026

**Working Group:** WG Identity & Trust

**Reporting period:** October 2026

**Chair(s):** Grant Miller (IBM) and Alper Dedeoğlu (SAP SE)

**Date submitted:** 2026-11-01

---

## Summary

October was a productive month centred on converging the Agent Identity Primitive toward a first mergeable draft. The WG refined key terminology (accountable party, responsible party, sub-agent, root authority) across the reference architecture and taxonomy, received a live demonstration of agent identity in practice via the Vector network, and heard a new authority-delegation model from Kevin grounded in ISO/IEEE 42010. The draft is ready to merge and will be submitted to the TC for review as the month's primary deliverable.

## Progress Against Objectives

- **Agent Identity Primitive:** Reached a reviewable first draft. Terminology was normalised across the document — principal, accountable party, provider, relying party, responsible party, and root authority — and the draft was reviewed for alignment with taxonomy wording. Identity delegation was scoped out as a separate primitive to keep the current document focused. The draft is ready to merge into the repository and will be submitted to the TC for feedback before an official first version is released.

- **Reference Architecture:** Kevin presented an ISO/IEEE 42010-based authority-and-delegation model, introducing two new roles (approver and auditor) and six operational principles, including that authority only narrows during delegation and that every action must trace to a human principal and a controller organisation. The group identified open questions around multi-hop delegation, cryptographic binding of credentials to identifiers, and the distinction between an agent's creation lineage and its operational delegation hierarchy. All attendees were asked to review the draft and submit actionable comments.

- **Taxonomy alignment:** The sub-agent definition was aligned with the Taxonomy WG. The agreed definition scopes sub-agents by lifecycle (bounded to the parent agent) rather than infrastructure boundary, with the parent retaining the ability to stop the sub-agent. The definition is noted as a precision on the broader taxonomy definition, not a replacement.

- **Attestation:** Steven proposed a cross-working-group attestation team to address approval and intervention attestation requirements. Alper and Naveed expressed interest. A separate software attestation document is in early draft; formal working group formation is under discussion with the chairs.

- **External presentations and collaborations:**
  - Filip (Apex Fusion Foundation) demonstrated four agents deployed on Vector Mainnet — agent history tracker, document pipeline, audio transcription, and advocate — showing end-to-end identity registration, staking, and dispute resolution. Gaps identified: native delegation chains and DID document support are not yet implemented in Vector.
  - Jim reported that the DAWN working group within the IETF has been approved, covering agent discovery and naming — directly relevant to the WG's discovery metadata work.
  - Alfred (X402 Foundation) introduced their agent identity and payment protocol work; both groups agreed to share documents and coordinate a future presentation.

- **Process:** The group added a recurring 30-minute APAC-friendly meeting slot to improve participation across time zones.

## Blockers and Risks

- The OAuth 2.0 baseline question raised in the July report (whether to profile OAuth 2.0 as the normative authorization baseline for agents) has not yet received a TC response. This is now outstanding for three months and is blocking the WG from committing to a direction on authorization. An explicit TC position — even a deferral — would be useful.

## Decisions Needed from the TC

- **Agent Identity Primitive review:** The WG requests a TC review of the first draft of the Agent Identity Primitive before the group proceeds to an official versioned release. Feedback on scope, terminology, and any conflicts with other WG outputs is particularly welcome.

- **OAuth 2.0 authorization baseline (carried over from July):** Does the TC support profiling OAuth 2.0 as the normative baseline for how agents present authorization across platforms, with policy enforcement within backend systems explicitly out of scope?

## Next Month's Focus

1. Incorporate TC feedback on the Agent Identity Primitive and publish the official first version.
2. Identify the next primitive or gap to prioritise — candidates include alignment with SPIRE, OIDC, and the authority-delegation model from Kevin's reference architecture.
3. Advance the attestation working group proposal and define its scope and membership.
