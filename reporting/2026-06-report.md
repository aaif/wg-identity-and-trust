# [Report] Identity & Trust – July 2026

**Working Group:** WG Identity & Trust

**Reporting period:** July 2026

**Chair(s):** Grant Miller (IBM) and Alper Dedeoğlu (SAP SE)

**Date submitted:** 2026-08-04

## Summary

The Identity & Trust WG continued active development across three sub-working groups — Taxonomy, Reference Architecture, and Critical Use Cases — with meaningful convergence on core concepts including agent identity, delegation, action records, and consent receipts. The group evaluated NVIDIA's Secure Agent Workspace reference design as an external input to inform the reference architecture work. Foundational definitions and architectural assumptions are still being shaped before formal decision requests are raised to the TC.

## Progress Against Objectives

- **Taxonomy (sub-WG):** Active whiteboard collaboration produced candidate definitions for key terms including Agent, Action Record, Audit Log, Consent Receipt, and Human-in-the-Loop. Notable contribution proposing the Action Record as a distinct concept — a tamper-evident, content-private, independently verifiable record of a single consequential action — separate from observability/tracing and from identity/authorization artifacts. Boundary clarified: observability answers "what is my agent doing?" (for the operator); the Action Record answers "what did this agent verifiably do?" (for a counterparty, auditor, or court).

- **Reference Architecture (sub-WG):** Drafted a components table covering Discovery Metadata, Identifier, Identity, Authorization, Revocability, Evidence, Verification, and Reputation — each with definitions and candidate technologies/standards. Established the architectural assumption that sub-agents cannot hold more permissions than their delegating agent, which cannot exceed the initiating user's permissions.

- **Critical Use Cases (sub-WG):** Produced illustrative lifecycle use cases across three interaction patterns — Internal, B2B, and B2C — for agent naming, deployment, delegation, access control, audit/compliance, and retirement/revocation. Identified a detailed set of identity and trust functional requirements aligned with the WG mission statement, including verifiable delegation, dynamic per-request authorization, content-private action records with selective disclosure, and principal-attested revocation.

- **External input:** Evaluated NVIDIA's Secure Agent Workspace (SAW) reference design, covering its four-layer identity model (user/sponsor, workspace device, logical agent, tool call), delegation records, OpenShell policy enforcement sandbox, and the ODIS (Open Delegation and Identity Standard) open specification. Findings are being fed into the reference architecture and taxonomy work.

## Blockers and Risks

- No blockers so far.

## Decisions Needed from the TC

- **Authorization baseline:** The WG proposes to profile OAuth 2.0 as the normative baseline for how agents present authorization across platforms — covering token format, delegation chain, and scope expression. Policy enforcement within backend systems is explicitly out of scope; each system retains full autonomy over how it evaluates and enforces policy. This mirrors how OpenID Connect standardized identity presentation without dictating what applications do with it. Does the TC support this scoping?

## Next Month's Focus

1. Converge on a stable set of taxonomy definitions across sub-WGs, with particular focus on Action Record, Consent Receipt, delegation chain, and Human-in-the-Loop boundaries.
2. Advance the reference architecture component table toward a first reviewable draft, incorporating inputs from the NVIDIA SAW evaluation and Critical Use Cases.
3. Evaluate the OpenID Foundation whitepaper [Identity Management for Agentic AI](https://openid.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Identity-Management-for-Agentic-AI.pdf) for alignment with the WG's identity, delegation, and authorization work — particularly relevant to the OAuth baseline decision.
4. **Open call for industry presentations:** The WG is actively seeking presentations from industry players on their approaches to agent identity. Organizations working on agent identity standards, frameworks, or implementations — including but not limited to identity providers, platform vendors, and standards bodies — are invited to present to the WG. Interested parties should contact the WG chairs.
5. Begin identifying candidate standards gaps and potential TC decision points emerging from the architecture and taxonomy alignment work.
